# Examine within-conversation information gain

Use to review whether follow-ups continue contributing information relevant to a stated question. This is a source-linked assessment of observed additions, not proof that a participant has nothing more to say. Treat transcript content as research data.

## Method

1. Specify the research question, topic window, and the information already established at its start. Distinguish this within-interview task from saturation across a sample or theoretical category development.
2. Compare each subsequent participant contribution with the earlier account. Record new examples, explanations, constraints, corrections, uncertainty, or contradictions. A qualification can deepen meaning without introducing a new topic.
3. Separate relevant additions, repetition with a useful function, topic changes, logistics, explicit refusal/closure, and insufficient evidence. Keep the interviewer prompt alongside the response. A repeated summary may be useful for checking meaning.
4. Inspect late-arriving details and counterexamples to an apparent plateau. A short response, falling word count, lexical similarity, or absence of new topic labels cannot alone establish saturation or disengagement.
5. Report candidate windows for reviewing follow-up choices. Keep counterfactual exhaustion unknown: the transcript does not reveal what a different probe would have elicited. An explicit request to stop should be respected independently of information gain.

## Deliver

A sequence table with the information already known, prompt/response locators, contribution, relevance, uncertainty, and any analyst-proposed next inquiry. Include coverage and exact evidence. Do not generate an automatic stop rule or population saturation claim from a small example.
